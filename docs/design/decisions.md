# Design decisions

The designer has the final say on every gameplay decision. This file is the record of what has been ruled, so that work in later sessions starts from it. Newest rulings are at the bottom of each list. Where a ruling here differs from a proposal document, this file wins.

## The game

1. **One monster per lake.** Each lake has one target monster. The player investigates the lake, works out where it lives, when it feeds and what bait and rig it needs, and tries different areas and rigs. Catching it is hard and needs several things right at once. Landing it is what opens the next lake. The lake has plenty of other fish as well. The inspiration is River Monsters: one headline fish per episode.
2. **A fun, casual auto fisher.** Fights play out on their own. No dexterity tests and no simulation busywork.
3. **Mossmere first.** It should feel finished before work moves to any other lake. Sporewood stays as it is for now.
4. **Fish names follow real life.** A real kind of fish plus a descriptor (Reedjaw Pike, Mossback Perch). Mossmere's legend, formerly The Belled Matriarch, is the **Bellhead Catfish**, a wels. Her model and authored fight are kept.
5. **About 1 in 3 per cast** that the monster takes once every condition is right.
6. **Deep gear customisation**, made light enough to stay fun. The designer's tackle database (rod action, reel type, line material, leader, hook style, rig depth, bait or lure, chum, landing gear) is the source of ideas.
7. **The bait rule.** A fish only bites a bait or lure it would really take; one it would never take gives zero chance. A weak match (half chance) stays. Each fish's diet follows its real counterpart.
8. **Ordinary fish forgive on the rest of the rig; the monster does not.** Common fish bite on any sensible rig provided the bait or lure is one they would take.
9. **No boat hazards**, and none of the by-catch or environmental hazards from the tackle database.
10. **The designer decides; the build follows.** Gameplay ideas go to the designer first. When time is short, questions are put as quick yes or no choices and the remaining gaps are filled with the recommended option and reported.
11. **One voice.** All copy in the game follows the tone and style of the 10-step first-play guide: clear, very readable and nice to read. Short plain sentences, spoken to the player. The rules are in `voice.md`. Approved with it: fish lore describes the real animal in plain words; the tackle stat is called Grip, so "hold" only means the fish store; "Journal", never "log"; the old survival words (descent, trials, Shrine, Waystone) leave the screens.

## Mossmere, approved 21 November 2026

The proposals in this folder (`mossmere-hunt.md`, `mossmere-gear.md`) are approved with their recommended options, except where changed below.

- **Gear starts deeper than proposed (changes G1).** Mossmere gets a **leader** choice and a **hook** choice from the start, in addition to bait depth and live bait. See `mossmere-gear-deeper.md`.
- **Her reward (changes H8).** She is measured and released for the 900-clam bounty, and the player also gains a **completely unique item**. It gives **one unique bonus stat**, and it is **the only thing carried from lake to lake**.
- **Between lakes (changes G9).** Following from that: nothing else carries over. Rod, line and reel tiers, rig parts and clams all start fresh in a new lake; only the monster's unique item travels.
- Everything else stands as recommended: H1 Live Minnow at 1 in 3 and Worm Bunch at 1 in 6; H2 Waxed Silk holds her, advanced by Murk after the first loss; H3 she feeds any time after dark; H4 bait on the bottom; H5 three tries and one hook-up a night; H6 landing at best three in four, and a loss to a wrong part gives no advantage; H7 landing her opens Sporewood; H9 the Bell Pool is found through the case; H10 a self-filling Case page opened by the bell on the second evening; H11 nudges, never the answer; H12 the Mossbell Isle angler is named Hob and gives one line a night; H13 top weight trimmed to about 150 kg; G2 the rig as one picture; G3 the lead shifts who bites and never stops a fish; G4 a free tin of three live minnows that empties each evening; G5 a bell on the rod tip; G6 the first lead is Murk's gift; G7 three saved rigs; G8 a swirl every time and words once a night per part; G10 Thousandcast Pike takes a live minnow.

## Mossmere story direction, 21 November 2026

The designer's direction for her story and her hiding place. The details are in `mossmere-story.md` once written and approved.

- **She has a story.** The fish the player hunts has a story of her own, told in the game's one voice.
- **Her story is "The thief of the mere".** Things vanish off the water after dark: ducks, floats, Hob's whole rod. People blame the gloom. It is her, hunting at the top. The drowned harbour bell is only scenery. (Chosen over "the bell she wears" and "the bell she lives in". Sketch 2 below was approved as "the bell" before this choice, so it is reworked to mark her water out of the thief story.)
- **The midnight bell tells the time.** An old bell up in the city still rings at the stroke of midnight, and the strange disappearances happen at about then. The bell tells the player **when** to fish for her. It never tells them where. (This changes H3, which let her feed at any time after dark.)
- **Hob half remembers.** The angler on Mossbell Isle once hooked something huge and lost it. He cannot quite remember what it was. Talking to him gives the **first clue: an area of the lake**, not the exact spot.
- **Her water is nearly unmarked.** The place she lives has few discernible markings. The player finds it by putting together all the clues gathered. This changes how the Bell Pool is revealed (H9, and the mist and reveal in the approved hunt).
- **Challenging but still fun.**
- **The fisherman's notepad.** When the player gains a clue, a picture of it is drawn in a notepad. The player studies the pictures to work out what they are hunting and where. The notepad takes the place of the approved Case page (H10) as the record of the hunt. See `mossmere-notepad.md` once written and approved.
- **The notepad's look is approved:** a worn spiral pad, pencil sketches, a few handwritten words.
- **The notepad's three headings are Where, Suspects and Gear.** ("Suspects" replaces "Which fish it isn't", by the designer's ruling.)
- **Five sketches in total.** The notepad holds five sketches for the whole hunt, not one per small clue. Each is a picture worth studying.
- **Three questions.** The hunt is about three things: **where she is**, **which fish it isn't**, and **what gear will catch her**. The five sketches, approved in outline:
  1. *Hob's map* (where). Talk to Hob. He draws the lake and shades the part where he lost her.
  2. *The bell* (where). Stay out after dark in that part of the lake. A bell rings under the water and the player draws where the ripples start. That marks her spot.
  3. *The line-up* (which fish it isn't). The lake's big fish, drawn side by side. Each one caught is crossed out, with the reason it could not be her.
  4. *The swirl* (gear). Fishing her spot with worms under a float, something big turns below and leaves them. She wants a live fish, on the bottom.
  5. *What came back* (gear). Hook her and lose her. The broken end is drawn beside Hob's old one. It shows the hook, leader and line that will hold her.
- **She stays unknown.** While the player rules fish out one by one, nothing shows or says what she is. Her kind, name and picture stay hidden until she is caught.
- **Bite marks.** Fish the player catches sometimes carry a mark from whatever hunts them. The marks point to the kind of predator out there and help rule suspects out.
- **Clue ideas to draw on.** The designer's list of River Monsters clue types (wounds and scars, signs in the field, what witnesses say, local tales and history, red herrings) is the pool for this and later lakes. Not all of it is for Mossmere.

## Mossmere hunt, second version, approved 21 November 2026

The full design is `mossmere-hunt-2.md`. The notepad's look and how its sketches are made are in `mossmere-notepad.md`, with a tappable mock-up in `notepad-mockup.html`. It replaces the clue design of `mossmere-hunt.md` (the Case page, the bell at night). The designer's answers to its four questions:

- **She steals a fish off your line, float and all.** Once a night, just after the midnight bell, inside Hob's part of the lake, and only until her water is found. The float is replaced free. That theft is how her water is found, and it is what sketch 2 draws. (A narrow exception to rule 9.)
- **One part per loss.** A wrong rig can lose her up to three times, in fight order: hook, then leader, then line. Each loss teaches one part only. Sketch 5 is still one sketch and grows by one part with each loss. (The designer turned down "one loss shows every wrong part".)
- **The unique item is Hob's Lamp.** Hob gives it when she is landed. It hangs on the raft in every lake. Its one bonus: kinds of fish the player has never caught bite more often. It never helps against a lake's monster.
- **Going back.** An earlier lake's gear and clams wait as the player left them. Only the unique item travels.
- **The midnight cutscene.** At the first midnight after her water is found, a short cutscene plays: a grown water bird sits on the water, a boil rises under it, and it is gone. It runs about five seconds, can be skipped, and the evening's clock stops while it plays. It shows a dark shape and a swirl, never the fish. It plays once only. On later nights a splash and a ripple at her spot mark the start of her feeding time, with no cutscene. (This replaces the single bird she took from the Gatehouse flock in the design.)

Gaps filled by the team, as reported to the designer: the midnight bell hangs in the leaning toll-tower at the north end and strikes about two minutes after setting out; her feeding time lasts about three minutes from its first stroke; none of her signs appear before Hob has drawn his map; she is called "the thief" and "it" until she is landed; the notepad draws a new sketch as soon as it is gained; the five line-up fish are drawn from the start and the sixth frame stays a question mark.

## Adopted from an outside review, 21 November 2026

The designer showed the game's outline to an outside reviewer and passed these on as direction. How each is built is in `mossmere-review-addendum.md` once written.

- **Every rig condition can be worked out.** One part per loss stays as the fallback teacher. A sharp player who reads the clues (Hob's worn line end, Murk's shelf lines) can get the rig right in one or two attempts and is never forced through three losses.
- **Relics are for utility and discovery.** Every later monster's unique item helps the player find, see or handle things. None gives raw Grip or more clams. (Ideas only, for later lakes: signs seen from further away; one more live bait in the tin; a slightly wider clean cast or strike notch.)
- **Tackle arrives with the story.** One new thing an evening, strictly. New tackle is brought by story beats and sketches earned, not by fixed evening numbers, so the player always knows why an item appeared. Chafe Gut does not reach Murk's shelf before the player has seen Hob's worn line end.
- **Local fish variants.** A few more kinds of fish, each tied to one clear home in the lake, reusing existing models with their own colours and markings. Every minnow variant counts as a live minnow. Variants are extra Journal entries and do not lengthen the lake's main list. **Built after the hunt** (the designer's choice), as a first batch of three or four.
- **Murk's Order Board teaches the new rig.** Once a part is owned, the board sometimes posts a double-pay order that uses it on an ordinary fish.
- **Bad luck never walls a player.** Losing her on a fully right rig still makes her a little easier next time. Losing her to a wrong part gives nothing, so the puzzle still has to be solved.

## More rulings on the outside review, 21 November 2026

Details are in `mossmere-review-addendum.md`.

- **No credit for the silk (changes H2).** "You need to save up to buy the right rig." Murk never puts Waxed Silk on his slate, neither when her water is found nor after a line loss. The player saves clams and buys it from the line shelf. Any mention of the slate, credit or an advance for the silk in the design documents is withdrawn.
- **Earnings must be measured.** Because the silk has to be saved for, how many evenings it takes to earn its price is now part of the hunt's length. It is to be measured in play, and the price brought to the designer if it makes the hunt drag.
- **The relic test.** A later monster's item passes only if it changes what the player finds, sees or carries or how they handle the rig; never adds Grip, landing odds or clams; never touches a lake's monster; stays small and does not stack; is felt on the first evening in a new lake; and can be said in one plain line. (The outside review's "wider strike notch" idea fails as worded, because a clean strike adds Grip.)

## Rulings during the build, 22 November 2026

- **Not every Kissmark Trout has been in her mouth.** About one Kissmark in four carries the wide ring of fine scrapes, not all of them. The trout is named for a natural round pale mark on its cheek, and its lore says so. In the line-up, a Kissmark landed with the ring is crossed out with "It has the ring of scrapes on it. Something had it, and let go."; one without it is crossed out with "It is big, but its mouth could not take a bird." Fish landed near her water in her time still carry the ring about half the time. (This changes the bite-marks table in `mossmere-hunt-2.md`, which gave the ring to every Kissmark.)
- **The tale may share a tally with one of Murk's gifts.** The tale, the line-up and the find are story, not new tackle, so "one new thing a tally" does not hold them back.
- **The Waxed Silk stays at 600.** Earnings were measured on 22 November 2026 (prices checked against the game's own tally; bite rate and landing skill estimated; orders and finds left out). A casual player earns about 25 clams an evening and a keen one about 45, or about 80 once on Braided Gut. The full rig costs 800 (Braided Gut 100, which Murk needs before he shelves the Silk, the Turnback Hook 40, Chafe Gut 60, Waxed Silk 600), so a keen player has it on about evening 11 or 12 and a casual player on about evening 24. A lower price of 300 was offered and the designer said no: the player saves up for the right rig.
- **The three rigs are the player's own.** Nothing fills a rig for them. Murk's Sounding Lead goes in the kit and the tally says how to fit it; it no longer sets up Rig 2 (this replaces "Rig 2 is filled automatically by the lead gift" in `mossmere-gear.md`). A part the player buys still goes on the rig in use.
- **The Journal shows where a fish feeds** (top or bottom) once it has been caught. Approved.
- **Each lake keeps its own kit and purse (built).** A tow sets aside the leaving lake's tackle tiers, bought pieces, creels, raft parts, rig parts, rigs and clams, and brings in the arriving lake's. A lake never visited starts from a new game's kit with no clams. Hob's Lamp, found tackle, finds, the journal, the hunt record, looks, charts and the home's buys travel. Bait and lures were already per lake. Murk's shelf now counts from each lake's own tiers, which closes the shelf fault that was listed under "Still open".
  - Gaps filled during the build: found tackle keeps travelling, as the leaving card always promised. An old save that had towed and come back to Mossmere gets Mossmere's own tiers back from the tow's record, and what it bought on Sporewood's shelf waits in Sporewood; its clams stay where the barge is.
  - Not measured: earning in Sporewood from no clams and plain tackle. The first tackle step there costs 600.
