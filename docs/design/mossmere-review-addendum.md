# Mossmere hunt: the review addendum

> **Decided after this was written.** The designer ruled: "You need to save up to buy the right rig." **Murk gives no credit for Waxed Silk**, neither from the night her water is found nor after a line loss. Wherever this document or an earlier one mentions the slate, credit or an advance for the silk, read: the silk is bought with saved clams from the ordinary line shelf. A sharp player's fastest route is therefore as fast as they can save for it. The local variants are built straight after the hunt (also decided).


## The short version

- **Every part of her rig has a fair clue before the first hook-up.** Only two lines change: the ring-of-scrapes line on caught fish now says "a soft, rough mouth", and the Turnback Hook's shelf line now says "for soft mouths". The three losses stay exactly as approved, as the teacher for anyone who misses the clues.
- **A sharp player can fish the full rig on evening 5,** for 100 clams (the Turnback Hook and Chafe Gut). Murk offers the Waxed Silk on his slate the night her water is found (question 1).
- **Tackle comes with the story.** Each tally brings one new part at most, and Murk always says why. Chafe Gut never comes before Hob's worn line end. The live minnow tin and the Turnback Hook can also come from ordinary fishing, or on a tally where nothing else is due, so nobody is locked out.
- **Murk's Order Board posts six quiet practice orders,** one at a time, at the usual double pay, for players who own a board.
- **Bad luck:** losing her on a fully right rig gives her one wound, as the code does today. That means +6 Grip and a little more time to strike, up to three wounds. One new sentence tells the player. A wrong part gives nothing.
- **Relics are tested against five checks.** Hob's Lamp passes. Of the three ideas for later lakes, two pass and the strike notch fails as worded.
- **Local variants:** I propose four, built after the hunt (question 2).

## 1. Every part can be worked out before a loss

These are the clues a careful player has before the first hook-up. "New" marks the only additions.

- **Where.** Sketch 1, Hob's shaded map: "Hob lost it somewhere in this shaded part, between the Gatehouse and the Sisters." Then come the boils with their direction words, the scraped fish, and the theft (sketch 2). Nothing new.
- **When.** Murk tells the tale at the evening 2 tally: "It's always just after the old bell strikes midnight." At midnight: "This is when things go missing." Hob: "Both times it was just after the old bell struck midnight." Sketch 2 repeats it. Nothing new.
- **Bait.** Sketch 4 comes from a refusal, which is not a loss: "It wants something alive, down on the bottom." It shows an arrow down to a small fish. The Kissmark in the line-up was eaten at: "Something had it, and let go." Murk's tin line: "Perch eat little fish." Nothing new.
- **Depth.** Sketch 4 ("down on the bottom") and the lead's gift line. Nothing new.
- **Hook.** The theft: "Something grabs your fish and pulls it under." **New:** the ring line on caught fish now ends "Something big had it in a soft, rough mouth." **New:** the Turnback Hook's shelf line becomes "Hooks that set themselves. For soft mouths that grab and run." Two words now tie the clue to the part.
- **Leader.** Hob's line end, taped into sketch 1: "This is Hob's old line end. It is worn rough, not cut." The line-up pike: "Its teeth cut a line clean. Hob's line was worn, not cut." The ring has "no tooth holes", and **new**, it is "rough". The leaders' shelf line: "Wire for teeth. Thick gut for rough mouths." Pike Wire makes her refuse at the bite, not break free. Fair already. The one word helps.
- **Line.** Hob: "Another night it took my whole rod. It pulled it right over the side." The theft pulls a float under. Sketch 4: "Something huge." The rig picture: "the line is the limit." The Waxed Silk shelf line: "For the heaviest fish in the mere." **New:** Murk adds one sentence on the night of the find (section 2).

No line of Hob's changes, so his rule stands: he never gives what the sketches have not.

**The sharp player's fastest route.**
1. Evening 1: Murk gives the Sounding Lead.
2. Evening 2: a perch earns the tin. At the tally the tale sends you to Hob.
3. Evening 3: Hob draws his map. You wait in his part until midnight. The theft finds her water, and her time ends there, because she has just fed.
4. Tallies 3 and 4: the Turnback Hook and the leaders, one each. The slate is offered.
5. Evening 5: a live minnow on the lead, the Turnback Hook, Chafe Gut and Waxed Silk. That is the first try.
- Evening 4 shows boils but no take, because one part is still waiting to reach the shelf.
- **Shop check.** The lead and tin are free, and the silk goes on the slate. The hook (40) and gut (60) cost 100, reached by tally 4 at about 25 to 50 clams an evening (unmeasured) if nothing else is bought first. If playtests show players short, the slate could take those too.
- **Odds from there.** She takes on about 7 nights in 10. She is landed about one time in two on the starting rod and reel, and three in five after one dice loss. So one or two hook-ups.

## 2. Tackle arrives with the story

**The rule.** A new thing is a new piece of tackle, on the shelf or given. Each tally brings one at most, with one plain line from Murk. Story lines (the tale, the line-up, the find) and the slate are not new things, so they can share a tally.
- **Two due at once.** The part whose beat came first arrives, and a tie goes hook before leader. The other waits a tally and shows nothing early.
- **A quiet tally** has nothing due. On it the next ordinary part still waiting arrives anyway: the tin, then the Turnback Hook. So the first three tallies each bring something, hunt or no hunt.

**Nobody is locked out.** The tin and hook come on quiet tallies, and the leaders at the latest about two evenings after the tale, through the approved nudges. **Her first take waits until the Turnback Hook and Chafe Gut are both on the shelf.** Her boils, refusals and signs carry on as approved.

## 3. Murk's Order Board teaches the new parts

**The board today.** A home buy: 140 clams for one order, and the Rust Buoy-Bell adds one. Each order names a fish from the lake's list or a chart you own: one or more of a kind, a Prize size, or a set weight. A fish that fills it sells for double. A filled or wiped order is replaced, and Murk says "Bring me ..." once.

**Practice orders** are one more kind: one ordinary fish, taken on one part.
- **When and how often.** From the tally after you own the part. When a slot refills, it is a practice order 1 time in 2 until that part's order is filled, then 1 time in 6. One practice order at a time, one fish each.
- **Pay.** Double, like every order: a median Milkeye pays about 36. Nothing else is new. No board, no practice orders, and a fish taken the wrong way simply doesn't count.

## 4. Bad luck never walls a player

**What happens today** (checked in the code). Each time she gets away she gains one wound, unless the loss was to a line too light for her.
- Each wound adds 6 Grip to later fights with her, up to three. While she has one, you get a tenth of a second more to strike. Wounds last across evenings and are cleared when she is landed.
- The approved designs also give no wound for hook and leader losses. The theft is never her loss.
- **One wound** lifts her landing from about one in two to three in five on the starting rod and reel, and from three in five to two in three on the best.

**The rule.** A loss on a fully right rig is the dice. It gives her one wound, up to three. A loss to any wrong part gives none.

**What the player is told.** The approved dice line gains one sentence: "It shook free right under the raft. Everything held. It will be a little weaker next time." After the third wound the last sentence is left off. No number is shown, and her row stays off the Odds page until she is landed.

## 5. The relic rule for later monsters

A monster's unique item helps the player find, see or handle things. It passes only if all five are true:
1. It changes what you find, see or carry, or how you handle your rig. It never adds Grip, landing odds or clams to any catch, find or bounty.
2. It never touches any lake's monster: not her bite, her tries, her needs or her landing.
3. It is small and bounded. It fades by itself or has a small fixed cap, and it never stacks with another relic.
4. It is felt in a new lake's first evening.
5. It can be told in one plain line.

**Hob's Lamp passes all five.** Kinds of fish you have never caught bite more often. That is discovery. It never touches a monster, it fades as you meet each kind, and it is strongest on your first evening in a new lake. It can bring an unmet giant sooner, but it never changes what anything sells for.

**Ideas only, for later lakes.** Signs and boils seen from further away: passes. One more live bait in the tin: passes, because tries a night, not the tin, set a monster's odds. A slightly wider clean-strike notch: **fails as worded**, because a clean strike adds Grip. It would pass if it only made the notch easier to see.

## 6. Local fish variants (a proposal for after the hunt)

**What Mossmere has now.** Weeping and Nightbloom Carp are the only true pair: both are common carp, and the Nightbloom is a visitor, on the pads at night, with the Lily Gardener's Notebook. Kissmark and Rosefleck are two kinds of trout (a big brown trout and a visiting brook trout). Reedjaw and Thousandcast are a pike and a giant pike. Mossback and Milkeye are a perch and a zander. Visitors already sit outside the lake's list, so the precedent is there.

**The first batch.** Each reuses its base fish's model, with new colours and markings.

| Variant | Base model, and what differs | Its one home | Bites | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stone Minnow (a European minnow) | Dusk Minnow: olive back, dark blotches along the side, copper belly | the stony shallows only | day and dusk, as the Dusk Minnow | goes in the tin as a live minnow |
| Archshade Minnow (a sunbleak) | Dusk Minnow: smaller, a bright blue-green stripe, upturned mouth | Under the Span only | day, dusk and night, as the Dusk Minnow | goes in the tin as a live minnow |
| Mirror Carp (a real carp strain) | Weeping Carp: dark bare skin, a few big mirror scales, no tear streaks | the Lily Maze only | day and dusk, as the Weeping Carp, and the Worm Bunch | lands more on Chafe Gut, sets itself on the Turnback Hook |
| Buoy Rudd (a rudd) | Lampsipper: brassy gold sides, deep red fins, no moth spots | the Buoy Lights only, at night | as the Lampsipper | the night one |

- **The minnows** go in the tin as an ordinary live minnow, the same bait for the thief and every other fish. Nothing asks which minnow it was. I renamed the suggested "Bell-Shade Minnow", because "bell" would point at her bell, the reveal at the end.
- **The Journal.** A small "Local kinds" strip sits under each base fish once it is caught, with each uncaught variant shown as a "?". The lake's list, the catch seal and the Order Board leave variants out. Hob's Lamp counts them as new kinds, so after the hunt it helps you meet them.
- **Cost.** Medium in total. Each variant is small: a record, a colour and marking patch, a portrait, a lore line, a field guide row. The Journal strip is small to medium. Bite shares in those four homes change, so the seeded catch checks need a new baseline there.
- **What must not change.** The line-up stays five fish. Her needs and her one Live Minnow bait stay. The gate stays: landing her opens Sporewood. No variant's home may reach her water, and her marks stay as they are.

## Gaps I filled

- A "new thing" means tackle, so story lines and the slate are not counted. The order when two parts are due, and the quiet tally.
- On the find night her time ends at the theft, and her takes wait for her parts. Murk's handed-on map includes the line end.
- The slate can take Waxed Silk straight from Twine, skipping Braided Gut.
- Practice orders need a board (no free one), one at a time, at 1 in 2 then 1 in 6, one fish each.
- The dice line's last sentence is dropped after three wounds. "Bell-Shade" became Archshade, and variants stay off the board and the catch seal.
