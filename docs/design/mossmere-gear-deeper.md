# Mossmere gear, deeper: a leader, a hook, and one thing to keep

> **Read this first.** This addendum was written before the story was chosen. Three things in it are replaced by `mossmere-hunt-2.md` and `decisions.md`: the Case page and its eight headings (now the notepad's five sketches), Hob's seven fact lines, and the Harbour Clapper (the unique item is Hob's Lamp, with the same bonus). Its hook and leader parts, their prices and days, her needs and the rule of one part per loss all stand.


**The proposal.** Following the designer's ruling of 21 November, Mossmere gets two more rig parts from the start:
- **Leader**: the **Plain Knot** (everyone's rig today), **Pike Wire** and **Chafe Gut**. Its one job: *what can cut or wear through the end of your line.*
- **Hook**: the **Mere Hook** (today's) and the **Turnback Hook**. Its one job: *how surely a bite becomes a hooked fish.*

Neither part sets a size limit (that stays the line's job), neither turns any ordinary fish away, and both defaults play exactly as today. The Bellhead Catfish now also needs Chafe Gut and the Turnback Hook. "The line parted" becomes three things you can see on what comes back: a **bare hook**, a **frayed end**, a **snapped line**. Landing her also gives the **Harbour Clapper**, the one thing that travels to the next lake: fish you have never caught bite half again as often.

## How it plays

- **Evenings 1 and 2.** As approved. No leader or hook is shown or mentioned.
- **Tally of evening 3.** Murk shelves the Turnback Hook at 40: *"Hooks that set themselves. For the ones that grab and run."* Bought, it goes on the rig you are using, and a Hook part appears on the rig picture. A Weeping Carp takes and bolts, the rod pulls round by itself, and the card reads **Set itself · Clean**.
- **Tally of evening 4.** Pike Wire and Chafe Gut, 60 each: *"Wire for teeth. Thick gut for rough mouths. A plain knot for everything else."*
- **In the Bell Pool**, on the Plain Knot, she takes, rolls at the bell, and the line comes back **frayed at the hook**. On Chafe Gut next time, she runs for the bell and the Braided Gut **snaps at the rod tip**. Murk advances the Waxed Silk. The next take is the real fight.

## The design

### Parts and their one job

| Part | Its job | Mossmere options |
|---|---|---|
| Rod, Reel | land more of what you hook | as today |
| Line | how big a fish you can land | Twine, Braided Gut, Waxed Silk |
| **Leader** | what can cut or wear through the end | **Plain Knot, Pike Wire, Chafe Gut** |
| Depth | who bites | Cork Float, Sounding Lead |
| **Hook** | how surely a bite becomes a hooked fish | **Mere Hook, Turnback Hook** |
| Bait or lure | who bites | as approved |

### Leader

| | Plain Knot | Pike Wire | Chafe Gut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good for | every fish, as today; nothing is put off | toothy fish land more often: pike, the Thousandcast, Milkeyes | rough mouths and snag-runners land more often: carp, koi, bass, sturgeon, the lamprey |
| Costs you | teeth and rough lips can still part it, as today | wary fish come less often: chub, trout, Milkeyes | small, dainty biters come less often: Dusk Minnows, Lampsippers, darters |
| On the water | the line runs straight to the hook | a glint of brass wire above the hook, on the picture and every catch card | a short thick amber length above the hook |
| Price | owned | 60 | 60 |
| From | the start | tally of evening 4, or sooner after your first Reedjaw Pike or the case's first Leader line | the same |

It works with lures too: wire on a spinner is how pike are really fished. Once a part is on the shelf, an ordinary fish lost on the Plain Knot can say why, once a night: *"Bitten through. Pike Wire stops teeth."* or *"Worn through on its lips. Chafe Gut won't wear."* The odds behind those losses are today's.

### Hook

| | Mere Hook | Turnback Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Good for | every fish, as today | fish that grab and run are hooked **clean** every time, hard takes included: carp, koi, bass, pike, trout, sturgeon, eel |
| Costs you | a fish that bolts before the strike is only **struck**, as today | quick nibblers are never better than **struck**: minnows, chub, suckers, Lampsippers, darters |
| On the water | today's strike | the rod pulls round on its own with no strike, and the word *Set itself* |
| Price | owned | 40 |
| From | the start | tally of evening 3, or sooner after the case's first Hook line |

Hooks matter on bait only. Lures carry their own hooks, so with a lure fitted the Hook part merges into it, as Depth does.

### What they do to ordinary fish

They never stop a bite. Each fish has at most one tag per part (table in B3). A "lands more" tag adds Hold for that fish. A "comes less often" tag shifts its share of bites, never to zero. With all the new parts together, no fish falls below about a third of its share today.

**"The line parted", divided.** On the hook, parts are tested in fight order: hook, then leader, then line. The first wrong one ends it, and only that one is named.

| Wrong part | What happens | What comes back | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | a few seconds, then slack | a **bare hook** | "The strike pulled the hook straight out of her lips. It came back bare." |
| Leader | half the fight, then slack | a **frayed end** | "She rolled at the bell and the line went slack. It came back frayed at the hook, rasped through." |
| Line | the full fight | a **snapped line**, no leader | "She ran for the bell and snapped the Braided Gut at the rod tip. Only Waxed Silk will hold her." |

**Never punished twice for one mistake.**
1. One loss names one part.
2. Each part can cost you one hook-up in the whole hunt. After that, while it is still wrong, she refuses with a swirl instead: no fight, no try used. ("She mouthed the minnow and let it go. She remembers the iron hook.")
3. No wrong-part loss gives her a wound or you an advantage (as H6).
4. A part shown right keeps its brass tick and is never blamed later.

### The rig picture and saved rigs

- **Two new parts on the slice.** The leader is drawn just above the hook, and the hook is drawn at the bait. A part appears only once you own a second option for it; until then the line ends in today's plain hook.
- **The limit line is unchanged.** Leader and hook never set it.
- **"Here, now"** includes the leader's shifts.
- **A saved rig** now stores bait or lure, depth, leader and hook. Rod, reel and line are still always your best. Names stay short ("Bottom · Minnow"), and a non-default leader or hook shows as a small icon on the tab.
- **Buying** a leader or hook fits it to the rig you are using, and Murk says so: "On Rig 2 now."

### The first ten minutes, and the first hour

- **The first ten minutes are today's game.** The Plain Knot and Mere Hook change no number, no part is drawn, and no word about them appears until one is on the shelf.
- **After evening 2, one new part an evening, never two.** Hook on evening 3, leader on evening 4. Each comes with one line from Murk and appears on the picture only when you own it.
- **The defaults still catch every ordinary fish.** A player who never buys either loses nothing outside the Bell Pool.
- **Nothing wears out.** A leader or hook is never lost with a fish.
- **Every choice is named where it happens:** *Set itself*, a wire glint, a frayed end.

### Her unique item

| | **The Harbour Clapper** (recommended) | Hob's Old Hook | The Bellhead's Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | the small bronze clapper that tolled in her bell-skull. It works loose as you measure her | Hob's rusted hook, still in her jaw. Hob: "Keep it. It's yours now." | the knotted cord you measured her with |
| Where it shows | hung from the raft's lantern pole; it rings once when a new kind of fish takes | on the Wick's hat band | coiled on the raft rail |
| Its one stat | **First Meeting: fish you have never caught bite half again as often** | Hob's Luck: every fish sells for a tenth more | Reading the Water: signs on the water show half again as often |
| First evening in the next lake | every fish there is new, so it is felt at once | felt at the first tally | felt on the first casts |
| Later monsters | never touches a lake monster, and fades as you meet each fish | adds a little money, and nothing to any monster's needs | signs never point at a monster |

**Why the Clapper.** It is her bell. It is strongest exactly when you arrive somewhere new, and it fades by itself, so it cannot snowball. It helps the next case (suspects are fish you have not caught) without touching what the monster needs. In Mossmere it applies at once, helping you finish the fish you are still missing.

**The rule for travel.** Only the monster's item travels. Everything that changes how you fish starts fresh in each new lake: rod, reel and line tiers, rig parts (lead, tin, leaders, hooks), lures and bait, raft fittings and creel, and clams. Your record travels, because it changes no fishing: journal, cases, prints, keepsakes, looks, and the charts you have opened. Each lake keeps its own kit and purse, waiting as you left it.

**For Mossmere now.** The Clapper is granted the moment she is landed and works from the next cast. The 900 bounty goes into Mossmere's purse, for rematches and the home. At the tow, Mossmere's kit and purse are set aside and Sporewood starts from its own start kit. This replaces the approved "the lead and tin stay in your kit" (G9).

## Decisions for you

**1. Is the unique item the Harbour Clapper (fish you have never caught bite half again as often)?** Recommend **yes**: it is felt hardest on the first evening in a new lake, fades as you meet each fish, and never touches a monster.

**2. Can she be lost once for each wrong part (hook, then leader, then line), each loss naming one part?** Recommend **yes**: it is the show's lost-fish beat, and no mistake costs more than one night. If no, a wrong hook or leader makes her refuse at the bite instead, and the line stays the only loss.

**3. When you go back to an earlier lake, are its kit and clams waiting as you left them?** Recommend **yes**: "start fresh" then never takes away what you earned, and the bounty stays worth having.

**Decided without you, all reversible:**
- three leader options and two hook options;
- prices 60, 60 and 40, shelved at the tallies of evenings 4 and 3, sooner if the case points there;
- parts appear only once owned;
- nothing wears out;
- the leader works on lures and the hook does not;
- neither part sets a size limit;
- the silk advance follows the first line loss;
- raft fittings and creel start fresh with the rest;
- looks and records travel;
- the Clapper counts giants but never lake monsters;
- buying fits the part to the rig in use.

### B2. Data

- **Parts.** Extend `wo.rig.parts`:

  - `pike_wire: { slot: "leader", price: 30, shelf: { evening: 4, heading: "leader", caught: "reedjaw_pike" } }`;

  Shelf prices are ×2 (`wo.cost.factor`): 60, 60, 40. Set `water: "mossmere"` (kits are per lake now, B4). This replaces the gear appendix's `water: null`. The rule is "shelved from the earliest of the conditions met", checked at the tally. All parts are durable: never spent, never lost on an escape.
- **Leader tags.** Add `bs.mossmere.leader = { speciesId: { pike_wire: "toothy" | "wary" | ["toothy","wary"], chafe_gut: "rough" | "delicate" } }`; a missing tag is neutral. Add `wo.leaderFx = { toothy: { hold: 4 }, rough: { hold: 4 }, wary: { bite: 0.7 }, delicate: { bite: 0.7 } }`.
  - **Bite** multipliers apply in all three bite paths, as depth does (conditions §1.3): `l1` after `hook.mult[g]` and the depth multiplier; `_7` (`min(0.9, base × mult)`); `P7` guests and giants. Unlike depth, they apply to **lure casts too**, so `lq`'s odds (rebuilt through `Ce → G5 → l1`) pick them up with no change to `lq`.
  - **Combined floor.** The product of depth × leader multipliers has a floor of 0.35 (lead ×0.5 × leader ×0.7) and is never zero.
  - **Hold.** In `GP`, add `leaderFx[tag].hold` to hold when the fitted leader's tag for that species is toothy or rough. Not for legends: her leader is a gate (B5).
- **Hook tags.** Add `bs.mossmere.hookSet = { speciesId: "self" | "quick" }`; missing means neutral. On `turnback_hook`, bait or bare casts only:
  - "self": a strike grade of `struck` becomes `clean`, including the forced `struck` of a hard take (`rollHardTake` → `Nn("struck", null, true)`; conditions §1.7);
  - "quick": `clean` becomes `struck`.
  - Early, late, spooked and passed are untouched. The rule is applied in `Nn` before `GP` reads `strike`. `CATCH_V3.strike` is unchanged (clean 8, struck 2).
  - New word in the strike area: *Set itself* (self-setters on the Turnback).
- **Ordinary loss words.** In the `fish:escaped` listener, when not `overCap`, the fitted leader is `plain_knot`, the species is tagged toothy or rough, and that option is shelved or owned: show the matching line, once per night per tag. Turnback shelved, fitted hook Mere, hard take lost on a "self" fish: "It ran before the hook was set. A Turnback Hook sets itself." Text only; the odds are unchanged.
- **Monster gate (hunt A2, step 6).** The bite-time order becomes: lure → depth → bait → leader is `pike_wire` (refuse, part "leader") → lesson refusals for any part in `lessons` that is still wrong (hook, leader, line). Each emits `legend:refused { part }`. Lesson refusals and the wire refusal use no try and no hook-up.
- **Unique item.** `wo.relics = { harbour_clapper: { from: "belled_matriarch", stat: { unmetBite: 1.5 }, mount: "raft_lantern_pole" } }`. In `l1` (pool rows), `P7` (giants and guests) and `_7`, multiply by 1.5 when `!(meta.journal[id]?.caught > 0)` and the species is not `rarity: "legendary"`. Ring `clapper_ring` on `fishing:bite` for such a species.

### B7. Risks

1. **The hunt gets longer.** A player who misses the clues can spend two more hook-up nights (hook, leader) before the line lesson: about 1.4 evenings each at a 70% take rate. The estimate is landing at evening 8 to 12, untested. Tuning levers, in order: shelve the parts sooner, make Kissmark and Reedjaw come sooner, cut prices, then the designer's question 2 (refuse instead of lose).
2. **Overwhelm.** There are four new choices in about the first hour. The playtest (hunt A13) adds a pass condition: players can say in their own words what the hook and the leader are for. It also adds a fail condition: anyone who stops fishing ordinary water because they think the defaults are wrong.
3. **The hook rides on the strike grade.** If an auto fisher's clean rate is already near 100%, the Turnback's upside is felt only on hard takes. Measure the clean share per class first, and if needed move its effect to the hard take alone.
4. **Per-water purse.** Clams are read in many places (shelf, orders, the Long Wall, Murk's credit). Audit before building. If question 3 is no, this shrinks to a simple reset at the tow.
5. **Phone width.** There are now seven nodes. Leave no room for an eighth without a scrolling layout.
6. **The Clapper touches regression seeds.** It changes bite shares for unmet fish, so seeded comparisons must run with it off.
7. **Fishing liberties.** She refuses wire, she "remembers" a hook, and Hold +4 is the same for every toothy fish. All are flagged in B3.
