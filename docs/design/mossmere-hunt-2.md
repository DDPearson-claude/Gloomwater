# Mossmere hunt, part 2: the thief of the mere

## The short version

- **The tale.** Things go missing on Mossmere at night: birds, floats, once Hob's whole rod. People say the gloom takes them. It is a fish. She rises at midnight to snatch what moves on top, then takes it down to eat on the bottom.
- **When.** The old bell on the toll-tower, up on the old city wall at the north end, strikes midnight two minutes after you set out. Things go missing just after it. She hunts for about three minutes, and never at any other time. The bell tells you when, never where.
- **Five sketches in a notepad.** Where: (1) *Hob's map*, (2) *Taken at midnight*, the spot where she took a fish off your line. Suspects: (3) *The line-up*, five big fish crossed out one by one, with the bite marks you find. Gear: (4) *The swirl*, (5) *What came back*.
- **Her water is plain water.** Before midnight, and before you have Hob's map, nothing there is different. After the bell, boils and scraped fish lead you in. No mist, frame, glow, toll, dark patch or shop chart.
- **She stays unknown.** No name, picture, outline or card anywhere until she is landed. Everyone calls her "the thief".
- **Her bell** is Hob's old rod bell, caught in his line the night she stole his rod. You see it only when you measure her.
- **Her item is Hob's Lamp:** new kinds of fish bite more often, in every lake.
- **She can be lost once for each wrong part,** in fight order: hook, then leader, then line. Each loss names one part and draws it into sketch 5, which builds up on one page. The designer's four answers are at the end.

## 1. Her story

**The tale, told at the tally of evening 2.** The narrator: "Things go missing on Mossmere at night. Birds go, and floats, and once a whole rod. People say the gloom takes them." Murk: "It's always just after the old bell strikes midnight. Hob on Mossbell Isle says it's a fish. Go and ask him." The tale is told whether or not you were out at midnight.

**The truth.** It is a fish, the biggest in the mere. She lies on the bottom of a deep hole all day. At midnight she rises and takes anything that splashes on top: a bird, a fish on a line, a float bobbing on a bite. She takes it straight back down to eat on the bottom.

**Why she lives there.** The old harbour was dug deep for boats, and the hole is still the deepest water in the mere. It is quiet and dark, and close to where the water birds roost by the Gatehouse.

**What vanishes, and how you see it.** Three things, all on the water itself, and only once you have Hob's map:
- **A fish off your own line, float and all** (the designer's "mutilated by-catch"). Just after midnight, in her water, the first fish you hook is grabbed and pulled under. Your line comes back empty, and you tie on a spare float at no cost. At most once a night, and only until you have drawn sketch 2. This is the find.
- **Scraped fish.** Near her water, just after midnight, about half the fish you land carry her mark: a wide ring of fine scrapes. You keep and sell them as usual.
- **A bird, in a short cutscene.** At the first midnight after you have found her water, a cutscene plays once: a grown water bird sits on the water, a boil rises under it, and it is gone. It lasts about five seconds, you can skip it, and the evening's clock stops while it plays. It shows a dark shape and a swirl, never the fish. On later nights a splash and a ripple at her spot mark the start of her time, with no cutscene. On every night the birds roosting by the Gatehouse scatter at the first stroke, if you are near enough to see them.
- Hob's rod stays in the tale until it comes back up at the end.

**Who gets the blame.** "The gloom" today is the old survival game's night enemy. It survives in a few live lines, such as the Gloam Eel's lore, and the first-play guide never uses it. Here it appears only in the tale, lowercase, to mean the dark that comes down at night. The hunt proves it wrong, and the notepad's last line says so.

**The midnight bell.** At the north end of the mere, the Sunken Gate is a wall of the old city, and a tall leaning tower stands on it with two lit windows. That is the toll-tower. It stands beyond the north shore, about 65 to 70 m from her water, and it is the only tall tower in Mossmere (the Gatehouse has only a short broken column). The game already has a Toll-Tower Vane among its finds, and today a bell already rings twice at the start of every dusk. The story makes that the same old bell.
- **Heard and seen.** Twelve slow strokes, far off, about two seconds apart: today's dusk bell, rung lower. It is the same loudness everywhere, so it points nowhere. The tower's top window flares with each stroke. A line shows at the first stroke, for players with the sound off.
- **Where it sits in an evening.** There is no clock on screen today. Day lasts 40 seconds and dusk 20, then night lasts until you go home. Midnight strikes 60 seconds after dark falls, two minutes after you set out. Your hold starts with room for three fish, and that fills in about the same time, so the bell comes before a casual player turns for home. Night fishing fills the wait.
- **Her time.** From the first stroke she hunts for about three minutes. Boils, scrapes, the theft, her tries and her refusals all happen inside it. When it ends, one line says "The water goes still again.", so nobody waits for nothing.
- **This changes H3.** She fed at any time after dark. Now she feeds only in her time. Three tries and one hook-up a night still stand.
- **The harbour bell** over her water never rings and never shows until she is found. After that it stands over her hole, silent, as scenery.

**Her own bell.** Years ago, Hob had a brass bell hung on his rod, to wake him if a fish took in the night. She took his bait and pulled the whole rod over the side. In her hole the line wound round her head, the bell slid down it and caught, and the line rotted away. The bell stayed, and she grew round it. You learn this only at the end. The measuring card shows a brass bell grown into her head, with a scrap of old line round it. Then your next cast in her water brings up Hob's rod, with its bell gone. The bell on her model is about a hand across, so the text calls it a brass bell, never a tip bell.

**Hunting at the top, against bait on the bottom.** No ruling changes. She hunts at the top but eats on the bottom. Whatever she snatches up top she takes straight down, and a float pulling back makes her let go. So the one thing she stops and eats is a live fish already lying on the bottom. Sketch 4 says exactly that.

**How it comes out.** About one new thing an evening:

| Evening | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Today's tutorial. Murk gives the Sounding Lead (approved). |
| 2 | The midnight bell strikes if you are out. At the tally the tale is told, Murk sends you to Hob, and the notepad opens. Hob's lamp is lit from tonight. |
| 3 | You sail past Mossbell Isle. Hob draws his map (sketch 1) and gives you his old line end. At the tally Murk names five big fish (sketch 3). |
| 4 to 5 | After midnight in Hob's part: boils and scraped fish. Then a fish is taken off your line (sketch 2). At the next midnight the cutscene shows a bird taken. |
| 5 to 6 | Fishing the spot after the bell, something turns under your rig (sketch 4). Line-up fish are crossed out as you catch them. |
| 6 to 9 | A live minnow on the lead. She takes, and you lose her once for each wrong part, one a night: hook, then leader, then line (sketch 5). After the line loss, Murk puts Waxed Silk on the slate. |
| 7 to 12 | Landed, measured, the bell seen, released. Hob's Lamp. Hob's rod comes up from her hole. |

## 2. The notepad and the five sketches

The notepad is a Journal tab with five pages, one per sketch, under three headings: **Where**, **Suspects**, **Gear**. Each page shows a pencil drawing and a few handwritten words, always in whole sentences. A page not yet earned is blank. At the foot is one narrator line, **What to try next**, which always points to a place, a time or a person, never the answer. It replaces the approved Case page. Nothing on it needs operating.

**Nobody has to find it.** When a sketch is drawn, the line "You draw it in your notepad." shows on the water. At the next tally the notepad opens by itself at that page, and closes with one press. So a player who never opens the Journal still sees every sketch once.

**1. Hob's map** (where)
- *Drawn:* the mere in pencil, seen from above, with Mossbell Isle, the Gatehouse, the two Sisters and home labelled in Hob's hand. A shaded patch takes in the water between the Gatehouse and the Sisters, about an eighth of the lake. Her water lies wholly inside it, off to one side. Taped in the corner is Hob's old line end, its last finger's length worn white and furred.
- *Earned:* from evening 2, sail within about 12 m of Mossbell Isle while his lamp is lit, and he talks.
- *Reads:* the area to search, and that whatever broke Hob rubs a line through rather than cutting it.
- *Words:* "Hob lost it somewhere in this shaded part, between the Gatehouse and the Sisters." Under the line end: "This is Hob's old line end. It is worn rough, not cut."
- *If missed:* after one evening the notepad says where Hob is. After two, Murk passes the map on himself.

**2. Taken at midnight** (where, and when)
- *Drawn:* a close-up of the corner of Hob's map. An X marks where your float went under, with the rings of a boil round it. Small in the top corner is the leaning toll-tower and twelve tally strokes.
- *Earned:* only by the theft, inside her water during her time. She never takes or refuses a bait before her water is found (approved: the bite gate needs Where), so the picture always matches what happened.
- *Reads:* the exact spot, and that it happens just after the midnight bell.
- *Words:* "Something took a fish off the line, right here. It happened just after the bell struck midnight."
- *If missed:* see the nudges in section 4.

**3. The line-up** (suspects). See section 3.

**4. The swirl** (gear: bait and depth)
- *Drawn:* a slice through the water at the spot. Your rig is drawn as it was, from the rig picture's own icons. A huge dark swirl turns under it with no shape inside. An arrow curls from the swirl down to a small fish lying on the bottom.
- *Earned:* her first refusal in her water, in her time. The default rig of worms under a float gives the approved picture. Other wrong rigs give the same picture with their own words.
- *Reads:* she wants something alive, and she lives and eats on the bottom.
- *Words:* worms: "It left the worms. It wants something alive, down on the bottom." A live fish on a float: "It took the live fish, but let go when the float pulled. It feeds down on the bottom." A lure: "It turned away from the lure. It wants something real, down on the bottom." The Worm Bunch on the lead: "It mouthed the Worm Bunch and let it go. It wants something alive."
- *If missed:* skipped by a player who already fishes a live minnow on the lead. Otherwise any wrong cast there brings it within a night.

**5. What came back** (gear: hook, leader, line)
- *Drawn:* one page that builds up, one small drawing per loss, in fight order. Each loss draws in only the part that came back from it: the bare hook; your worn end beside Hob's old one, worn the same way; the line snapped by the rod. Each has one plain sentence. A part you never got wrong is simply never drawn as broken.
- *Earned:* started by your first hook-up that ends in a loss. Each later loss adds its own part.
- *Reads:* a bare hook means a soft mouth, so a hook that sets itself (the Turnback). A worn end like Hob's means rough lips, not teeth, so thick gut (Chafe Gut). A snapped line means it is heavier than that line (Waxed Silk, which Murk then puts on the slate).
- *Words:* one sentence per part, in the appendix.
- *If missed:* a player who lands her without a loss gets the page at the landing, titled "What held": your hook, your leader and your line drawn whole beside Hob's old end, with "Your hook, your leader and your line all held."

**Losing her, one part at a time** (the addendum's rule, now decided). She can be lost once for each wrong part, in fight order: hook, then leader, then line. She breaks free at the first wrong part, at the addendum's fight points (the hook early, the leader halfway, the line at the end), and each loss names that one part only. A part that has already cost one hook-up makes her turn away at the bite afterwards, while it is still wrong, instead of costing another. That uses no try and gives no fight. No wrong-part loss gives her a wound or you an advantage (H6). A loss on a fully right rig is the dice ("It shook free right under the raft. Everything held."). It adds nothing to sketch 5, or starts it as "What held" if it is still blank. Wire still makes her refuse at the bite (addendum).

## 4. Her water

**How it is marked today** (the story draft's audit, checked again): a mist bank and lift camera, the oak frame with its cracked bell and clapper, a chart card ("The moon looks in on the old bell.") on sale at 1,000, an ambient toll every 18 to 32 seconds that carries up to 30 m beyond the pool's edge, a glowing column over its centre, the approved ring out of the mist, and a mist refusal on casts. **Missed by both drafts:** the water itself is shaded darker over her hole. The water shader darkens a feathered disc about 9 m across round her centre, at a fixed point, and nowhere else in the lake. That is a dark patch anyone can see by day. All of these go until she is found. Also going: the lead's "The deepest water in the lake" word, which would let a player sweep for the spot. It returns once she is found, and so may the dark tone. Kept, because they are the lake's own shape and too faint to read: the deep hole in the depth map, the drop-off ring round it, the wind lane steering round it, and no signs over its middle.

**The search.** Her water is the circle within about 8 m of her centre, around 200 m². That is about a seventh of Hob's part. She takes baits within 6 m, as approved. Nothing below happens until you have Hob's map, so nobody stumbles on her water before the story points there.

| How close | What you see | When |

| Near the roost (about 30 m) | the birds by the Gatehouse scatter | at midnight |
| Within 25 m | boils. One rises every 20 to 30 seconds somewhere over her water, with a word that says which way: ahead, behind, left or right | her time only |
| Within 16 m | about half your catches carry the ring of scrapes | her time only |
| Within 8 m | the first fish you hook is taken, float and all | her time, once a night |

## 5. Hob

**Who he is.** Hob has fished off the rock on Mossbell Isle every evening for forty years. The thief broke his line once, out in his old boat. Another night it took his whole rod. He has never seen what it is, and he cannot quite remember the details.

**Meeting him.** From evening 2 a lamp burns on his rock. Sail within about 12 m and he calls out. The first talk is four short boxes, then he draws sketch 1 and gives you his old line end. After that he says one new line a night when you pass, skipping any that are out of date, then a greeting. He never names her kind, and no line of his gives an answer the sketches have not given. His lines are in the appendix.

**His rod ends the story.** After the landing, your next cast in her water snags his old rod, green with weed and with its bell gone. Take it to Mossbell Isle. From then on he fishes with it, and his last line closes the tale.

## 6. The unique item: Hob's Lamp

The night you land her, Murk hands it over at the tally: Hob rowed across and left it for you. It is the lamp Hob lit on his rock every night of the hunt. It hangs at the front of your raft and travels to every lake.

**Its one stat stays the addendum's: new kinds of fish bite more often** (half again, for any kind you have never caught, but never a lake monster). It is felt on your first evening in a new lake, it fades by itself as you meet each fish, and it never touches what a monster needs. Its story fits too: fish that don't know you come up to look at a strange light. The Harbour Clapper goes, because her bell is no longer a harbour bell.

## Gaps I filled

- The bell is on the toll-tower on the Sunken Gate, the only tall tower in Mossmere, standing on the old city wall. The hanging ruins overhead have no tower to show. Today's dusk bell becomes the same bell.
- Midnight is 60 seconds after dark falls, two minutes after setting out, so it comes about when a three-fish hold fills. Her time lasts about three minutes. Both numbers are for tuning.
- What vanishes: a fish and your float off your line (the float is replaced free), scraped fish, and one bird, taken in the midnight cutscene after her water is found. The text just says "birds".
- "The gloom" appears only in the tale, and the ending disproves it.
- Hob had two losses (a line, then a rod), so that his old end exists for sketch 5. Her bell was the brass bell on his rod, caught in his line.
- Her signs wait for Hob's map, and she never takes or refuses before the find, so sketch 2 always shows a theft.
- The line-up has five fish, and there are three bite marks.
- The notepad opens itself at the tally after a new sketch.

## Decided

1. **She takes a fish and your float off your line** to mark her water: yes. At most once a night, in her water just after midnight, until you find her.
2. **The unique item is Hob's Lamp,** with "new kinds of fish bite more often": yes.
3. **One part per loss,** not every wrong part at once: up to three losses, each teaching one part, hook, then leader, then line.
4. **An earlier lake's gear and clams wait for you** when you sail back: yes.
5. **The midnight cutscene:** yes, once only, at the first midnight after her water is found. About five seconds, skippable, the clock stopped, a dark shape and a swirl and never the fish. A grown bird, not a chick.

## Build order (the game stays playable after each step)

1. **Hide her** (small to medium): the giveaways table, the ambient toll, frame, glow, the dark water tone and the deepest-water word, and a scenario test that her name and kind never show before landing.
2. **The approved gear** with the addendum's hook and leader (sized there).
3. **Her time and the midnight bell** (medium): the twelve strokes, tower flare, birds, her three-minute window, her rules moved onto it, and the addendum's three losses, one part each, with lesson refusals.
4. **The notepad** (large, mostly art): the tab, five pages, the swirl variants, sketch 5's three parts and its "What held" page, the tally opening, the "What to try next" line.
5. **Hob** (medium): the lamp, the talk, his lines, the map.
6. **Her water** (medium): boils with direction words, the ring marks, the theft, the find, the marker cork and chart ring, and the once-only midnight cutscene of the bird with the later nights' splash and ripple.
7. **The line-up and the marks** (small to medium): catch-card marks, crossings out, Murk's line.
8. **The ending, nudges and old saves** (small to medium): the measuring card, the rod find, Hob's Lamp and its stat, the Sporewood seal, the full-hold line. Then a timed playtest.

**How long.** Untested estimate: sketch 1 on evening 3, sketch 2 on evening 4 or 5, first hook-up on evening 6 or 7, landing on evening 7 to 10, and up to two evenings later for a player who loses her on all three parts. An evening that stays through her time lasts about five to six minutes.

## Risks

1. **Casual players go home before midnight.** The hold holds three fish and the guide says press HOME. Midnight now comes at about the time the hold fills, and the full-hold line says the bell is near. Measure when players leave. Further levers: an earlier midnight, then the nudge after one evening.
2. **Boils at night may be hard to see.** The direction words must carry them on a phone with the sound off.
3. **Losing a fish may feel unfair.** It is once a night at most, only until found, the float comes back free, and the line says it was the thief.
4. **Three tries in three minutes may be tight** if ordinary bites fill the casts, and water fished all evening bites more slowly. Lengthen her time before cutting tries.
5. **Leaks of her name.** Lore is copied in the field guide data and in hidden spare strings. Test for her name and kind, not just the screens.
6. **Art load.** There are five sketches plus variants and three marks. Pencil style keeps each small.
7. **The hunt length** (7 to 12 evenings) and the evening's length are untested.
