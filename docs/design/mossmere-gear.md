# Mossmere gear: one new part, one new bait, one picture

> **Two promises here were overruled** (see `decisions.md`, "Rulings during the build, 22 November 2026"). Nothing fills a saved rig for the player: Murk's Sounding Lead goes in the kit and the tally says how to fit it. Each lake keeps its own tackle and clams. The rest stands as approved, with the changes `decisions.md` lists.

**The proposal.** Mossmere gets one new rig part, **Depth**, with two options:
- the **Cork Float**, which is everyone's rig today;
- the **Sounding Lead**, which lays the bait on the bottom and tells you how deep it is.

It also gets one new bait, the **Live Minnow**, which you catch yourself. Heartmoss becomes the **Worm Bunch**, an ordinary big bait. The rig is shown as **one picture**, a slice through the water, and three saved rigs swap with one tap. The default rig plays exactly as today. The Bellhead Catfish tests both new choices plus Waxed Silk. One piece is large (the picture); the main risk is moving bite signals from the float to the rod tip.

## How it plays

- **Evening 1.** Today's game. The tutorial shows the rig picture once: rod, reel, line, cork, worms.
- **First tally.** Murk hands over a Sounding Lead: *"Not every fish looks up. A lead takes your worm down to the ones that don't."* It goes in your kit, and the tally says how to fit it.
- **Evening 2.** One tap on the rig chip fits Rig 2. The float vanishes. The line slants into the water, and a small brass bell sits on the rod tip. After the cast a word appears: *Deep*. Crayfish and Rustfin Suckers come more often. The tip knocks, the bell tinkles, and the rod pulls round.
- A Dusk Minnow's catch card shows **Into the tin (3 left)**. With a live minnow on, the cork wanders as it swims. A Mossback Perch takes it, then a Reedjaw Pike.
- In the Bell Pool after dark the rig fails. That part gets a red notch on the picture, and the swirl on the water says why.

## The design

### Parts and their one job

| Part | Its job | Mossmere options |
|---|---|---|
| Rod | land more of what you hook | Willow, Ashwood, Whalebone (as today) |
| Reel | land more of what you hook | Wooden Winch, Brass Spool, Clockwork (as today) |
| Line | how big a fish you can land | Twine, Braided Gut, Waxed Silk (as today) |
| **Depth** | who bites | **Cork Float, Sounding Lead** |
| Bait or lure | who bites | today's baits and lures, plus **Live Minnow** and **Worm Bunch** |

No hook part is shown. Each bait comes on its own hook.

### Depth

| | Cork Float | Sounding Lead |
|---|---|---|
| Good for | every fish, as today; you see every nibble | bottom feeders come more often |
| Costs you | nothing | fish that feed higher come less often; no float to watch |
| On the water | today's orange float | slanted line, a brass Tip Bell that rings on a bite, and a word for the depth: Shallow, Middling, Deep, or "The deepest water in the lake" |
| Price | owned | Murk's gift at the first tally |

The lead never stops a fish biting; it only changes who comes.
- **Bottom feeders:** crayfish, Rustfin Sucker, carp, Milkeye Perch, Bridgechain Sturgeon, Gloam Eel.
- **Higher feeders:** Dusk Minnow, Lampsipper, Reedjaw Pike.
- **Others:** unchanged.

With a lure fitted, the Depth part merges into the lure: "Lures swim at their own depth."

### Live Minnow
Land a small Dusk Minnow and tap **Into the tin**. Holding *Let go* still gives Cut Strip. The tin holds three and empties at the end of each outing.
- **Good for:** hunters such as perch, pike, bass, Milkeye and Kissmark, and it helps you land them.
- **Costs you:** you must catch it first, and grazers ignore it.
- **On the water:** the cork wanders, or the tip quivers on the lead.

### Worm Bunch (today's Heartmoss Ball)
"A fistful of worms on a big hook. Too much for little mouths." It still costs 20 clams for 10. Carp, suckers, crayfish and sturgeon take it gladly. Small fish mostly leave it.

### The rig picture
- **The view.** A slice of water with each part drawn where it sits: the cork in mid-water, the lead on the bottom.
- **Tap a part** to see its options, each with "Good for", "Costs you" and a price.
- **One line names your limit:** "Lands up to Trophy: the line is the limit."
- **A "Here, now" strip** shows up to five fish you have caught that would bite this rig here, now.
- **No numbers.** The Odds page keeps those.
- **Where it lives.** It replaces the tackle rows of Taper's Sheet and Murk's Tackle tab.

### Saved rigs
There are three saved rigs, named from their contents, such as "Bottom · Minnow". Murk saves one whenever you get a new part, and the HUD chip switches between them with one tap. A rig stores only bait or lure and depth. Rod, reel and line are always your best, so a saved rig never weakens you.

### When something fails
If a part is wrong, the catfish swirls every time. Once a night for each part, a line names it. The failed part gets a red notch on the picture, and a part proven right gets a brass tick. Catch cards show the rig used. All of this text comes from the same tables as the rules, so hints cannot go stale. Ordinary fish never fail outright on the new parts.

### Prices and other lakes
The hunt's gear costs nothing new except Waxed Silk at 600. Today, reaching her costs 1,020 (the Bell Pool plus Heartmoss). The lead and tin stay in your kit, but Sporewood ignores them until it is reworked. Strength tiers still reset on the first tow. Murk's shelf stops selling Sporewood tackle in Mossmere. Each later part will add one column, one node and one set of refusal lines, and its default will change nothing.

## Decisions for you

**G1. How much new gear Mossmere teaches.** (a) Depth and Live Minnow; (b) those plus a leader; (c) Depth only. Does (a) feel like the deep gear you asked for? **Recommend (a):** both are visible and both are needed by a wels. Of the orchestrator's open ideas, the lead covers the sounding weight and the Worm Bunch covers "big tackle puts off small fish". A leader suits Sporewood's toothy fish, and chum and hooks can wait for later lakes.

**G2. The rig screen.** (a) one picture as a slice of water, replacing the Sheet's tackle rows; (b) a strip added to today's Sheet. **Recommend (a):** it answers "hard to tell at a glance".

**G3. What the lead does to ordinary fish.** (a) shifts who comes, never stopping one; (b) nothing; (c) wrong depth refuses. **Recommend (a):** (b) makes it a one-fish item; (c) breaks your forgiving rule.

**G4. Live minnows.** (a) a card button and a free tin of three that empties each evening; (b) the same, plus a Bait Well raft fitting (120) that keeps them overnight; (c) buy them from Murk. **Recommend (a):** lightest; (c) removes "catch your bait".

**G5. The bottom bite signal.** (a) a Tip Bell and a rod-tip nod; (b) a small float kept on the surface. **Recommend (a):** you can hear it, and it suits a bell-haunted lake.

**G6. The first lead.** (a) Murk's gift at the first tally; (b) 60 clams from evening 2. **Recommend (a):** the first new choice costs nothing to try.

**G7. Saved rigs.** (a) three; (b) four; (c) none yet. **Recommend (a):** an earning rig and a hunting rig, with room for one more.

**G8. Wrong-part messages.** (a) a swirl every time, words once a night per part; (b) words every time. **Recommend (a):** (b) gets repetitive.

**G9. Gear between lakes.** (a) the lead and tin are kept, tiers reset as today; (b) everything resets; (c) nothing resets. **Recommend (a).**

**G10. Two diet fixes.** (a) Thousandcast Pike takes a live minnow, and the Gloam Eel stays moss-only for now; (b) neither; (c) both, with the eel moved to worms and small fish. **Recommend (a):** a live fish is the real answer to a lure-shy pike.

### B5. The lead on the water, and the tells

- **No float mesh.** The hide path already exists for lures (conditions §2.1, "orange halo is hidden when a lure is fitted").
- **Line.** A slanted segment from rod tip to bob that slackens over 0.5 to 2 s, scaled by `i.depth`.
- **Depth word.** It reads `ye(x,z)` (conditions §4.1). `DEPTH_BANDS`: Shallow below 0.35, Middling 0.35 to 0.75, Deep 0.75 and above. "The deepest water in the lake" shows only inside the Bell Pool disc. [A] The Bell Pool (depth 0.8 to 1) is the deepest named water; open-water depth values were not dumped.
- **Tip Bell.** A clip-on bell at the rod tip on every rod model. Reuse the Harbour-Bell Spinner's swinging-bell physics (handoff "Harbour-Bell Spinner"). It needs new tinkle and ring sounds.
- **Tells to move to the rod tip** (conditions §1.4, §1.8):
  - nibbles become a tip knock with a tinkle;
  - the tell or dome becomes a slow low nod (her two slow nibbles plus the dome);
  - the bite becomes the tip pulling round with a hard ring;
  - a hard take becomes a slam.
- **Signs and sight casts** still work with the lead; their responses play on the tip.
- [A] The bite schedule (nibbles, tell, strike window, hard-take rate) is unchanged. Only the picture and sound move.

- **Layout.** A portrait card that fits phone width. Nodes for rod, reel, line, depth and bait or lure sit where they are in the water.
- **Tray.** Each option card shows a plate, its name, "Good for" and "Costs you", plus Owned or a price. Unowned options sit behind "More at Murk's" on the raft and can be bought in the tray at Murk's.
- **Limit line.** It reads `Bd` and its existing `by` field (conditions §2.3).

- **Red notch and brass tick.** They come from `legend:refused` and from a take.
- **Where it lives.** It replaces the Sheet's Tackle page (`pages = { buffs, tackle, raft, home, odds, rivals }`; clues §7) and Murk's Tackle tab. The Overview, Raft, Home, Odds and Rivals pages stay.

### B9. Risks

1. **Tells built for a float.** This is the biggest feel risk. Every tell needs a rod-tip version, or the lead will feel mute. Test it on the handheld.
2. **Bite shares move on the lead.** Crayfish and suckers flood a bottom rig. Run the seeded 100-cast comparison per area and place (handoff "Fish interest"), on the lead and the cork. The cork must match today exactly.
3. **The tin button crowds the catch card.** Show it only on Dusk Minnows and only while the tin has room.
4. **Worm Bunch change.** Heartmoss is woven into legend code: `oe()`, `consumeOnCast`, refunds and the Odds sheet's legend row (legend §2.5, §5). Test refunds, the Odds page and old saves holding Heartmoss.
5. **Phone-width layout.** Five nodes, the limit line and the strip must fit portrait. Leave room for one future node.
6. **Scope creep on the Sheet.** Only the Tackle page is replaced.
7. **Fishing liberties.** A bottom-only wels that ignores lures, the rudd as a night feeder and the moss-eating eel, all flagged in B3. Tip bells for night bottom fishing are real practice [K, confident].
