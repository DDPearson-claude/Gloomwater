# Gloomwater's voice

## The short version

- **The voice is the ten-step guide.** A friendly narrator tells you what to press and what will happen, in short, plain sentences. Everything else is left out.
- **Measured:** about 10 words a sentence, never more than 25. Nine words in ten have one syllable. No semicolons, dashes or symbols.
- **Eight rules** follow. Every line in the game should pass all eight.
- **Characters keep a little personality.** Murk is dry and brief. Hob is warm and forgetful, but his clue is always plain. **Mystery** comes from what is left out and from real animal detail, never from fancy words. Her water gets plain text with no landmarks.
- **Mossmere has about 470 strings (2,900 words)** a player can read. Most are close already. Three groups are furthest away: fish lore and hints, the Odds sheet, and the title and new-game screens, which still talk about the old survival game.
- **The copy pass:** seven small reviews. Wrong facts come first, then the first hour, and the title screens come last. The four decisions at the end are approved.

## 1. Why the guide works

- **Who speaks.** A narrator, never a character, talks to the player as "you". Present tense. It points at what is on screen: "This is your chart", "This tin". Murk is "he".
- **Shape.** Each box has two or three sentences and 19 to 29 words. Three boxes open with a short reaction ("Welcome!", "Hooked!", "That one got away!"). Then come an instruction and its result.
- **Instructions.** One verb for each action: press, hold, let go, sail, wait. Buttons are written as they appear on screen, in capitals: SAIL, HOME, MURK.
- **Results, not numbers.** "Better rods and lines land big fish more often." The only number in the guide is the one on screen, and it is explained: "(the number is what he'll pay)".
- **Two-part explainers.** A colon joins a thing to how it is used: "Worms are bait: cast and wait." **Reassurance.** "You can't lose." "Just watch." A loss is told as a cause and a fix, never as blame.
- **Left out:** lore, history, odds, stat names, exceptions. The guide never says "Hold" as a stat, never gives a percentage, and never mentions the Gloom.
- **Measured** (appendix A1): 259 words, 27 sentences, 9.6 words a sentence, median 9, longest 25. 90% of words have one syllable and none has three or more. Punctuation: 24 full stops, 16 commas, 3 colons, 3 exclamation marks, 3 apostrophes, one pair of brackets. There are no question marks, semicolons, dashes, ellipses or quotation marks.

## 2. The rules

1. **Talk to the player.** Use "you" and the present tense, and point at what is on screen.
   Do: "This tin changes what's on your hook." Don't: "Begin where a Waystone remembers."
2. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for 6 to 12 words and never go over 25. Use two or three sentences per box.
   Do: "Hooked! The fight plays out by itself, so just watch." Don't: "Notched pads in the lee of Mossbell Isle, and koi glowing under them like lanterns under snow."
3. **Everyday words.** Most words should have one syllable. Use a long word only when it is a name.
   Do: "Big fish can break free, and some are too big for your gear." Don't: "Begins a new descent and abandons the waiting one."
4. **Name controls exactly.** Use one verb per action and write buttons as they appear on screen.
   Do: "Press HOME to sail back and sell your catch to Murk." Don't: "Hold on the water. Let go to cast." beside "Press on the water. Let go to cast."
5. **Say what happens, not the number.** Show a number only when it is on screen, and say what it means.
   Do: "Better rods and lines land big fish more often." Don't: "+{hold} Hold on every fish · counts ×1.6 against {styles}."
6. **One word, one meaning.** Keep to the glossary in appendix A4.
   Do: "Your hold only fits a few fish." Don't: "+{n} Hold." on the rod screen, or "Odds in your log" when the tab says Journal.
7. **Kind, never blaming.** A short reaction, then the cause, then the fix.
   Do: "That one got away! Big fish can break free…" Don't: "Too keen. It felt you. Again." or "That will not do."
8. **Plain punctuation.** Use full stops and commas, and a colon to show how. Allow at most one exclamation mark per box, only to open a moment. No semicolons, dashes or ×, → or + in sentences.
   Do: "Worms are bait: cast and wait." Don't: "A clean strike adds a little; an early or late one costs a little."

## 3. Characters and mood

All text is in the narrator's voice unless a character says it. A character may add **a little personality**: a dry joke, a habit, a pause. A character must **not lose** the rules: short, plain, and readable at a glance. A character's line always ends on something the player can use.

| Kind of text | How it sits in the voice |
|---|---|
| Guide, tips, labels | Narrator. Labels use sentence case ("Got it", "Skip the guide"), one to three words |
| Murk | Dry, clipped shopkeeper. Fragments are fine. One or two sentences, ending on a pointer: "Small ones make good bait. Press and hold “Let go”." |
| Hob (new) | Warm, forgetful old angler. First person, past tense. He may trail off once in a line, but never on the clue. For example: "I had something on once. Huge, it was. Out in the deep water, I think. Never saw it." |
| Fish lore | Narrator, one or two sentences about the real animal: its look, its habit, its size. One small touch of the drowned kingdom is fine, and so is a dry joke ("It looks like a chub. Nobody has checked.") |
| Hints and clues | Narrator, in whole sentences: where, when, on what. No "Sign:" shorthand. Never more than the clue knows |
| Places | One sentence about what you can see. **Her water gets no landmark** and no "you can see her" |
| Items and finds | Name, then one plain line on what it does. A one-line story may follow ("It rang the boats home once.") |
| Catch cards, toasts, losses | One sentence, subject first: "It threw the hook." Then the fix, as in rule 7 |

**Mystery without fantasy.** The lake is quiet and misty, and she is a real wels: whiskers, a mouth wider than your hand, bronze skin, night feeding, the bottom. It works like this:
- **Say what was seen, not what it means:** "Something rose under the cork, looked, and went back down."
- **Leave her unnamed** until the landing ("Something ancient."), and **use sound and dark:** a bell after dark, a ring on the water, a line that came back frayed.

No fish *is* the Gloom, has fins that burn without heat, or is made of frozen rain. No adjective should be doing the work a fact could do.

## 5. The copy pass

Each piece is one review: a before and after table of about 40 lines at most, and one commit. The game stays the same apart from its words.

1. **Wrong facts** (about 15 lines): the stale hints, the Heartmoss line, Murk's line for her, the page description.
2. **The first hour** (about 60, in two reviews): intro tips, the two cast tips, Murk's first lines, catch card, escape and gear-limit lines.
3. **The shop** (about 50 that change; names stay): shelf lines and Wick choice, plus the stat word if question 2 is yes.
4. **Journal and Odds sheet** (about 65): table text and the strings written into the screen code.
5. **Fish lore and hints** (54, in two reviews of ten fish): the field guide page changes in the same commit.
6. **Places, map lines and finds** (about 75): her water's lines are written with the new story, not before it.
7. **Title, menu and settings** (about 45): last, because the tests press some of these buttons.

New copy (Hob, the Case page, refusals, the three losses, the unique item) is written to these rules from the start and reviewed with its own build step.

**Must not change:** ids, save keys and the names the code looks text up by. Placeholders such as {name} and {class} keep the same names and number. Lists keep their length. The intro film stays under 60 characters a line, and first-evening Murk lines under 90. The buttons the tests press and the Wick names stay. The name "Bellhead Catfish" stays, and "Matriarch" must appear nowhere. Words a clue depends on stay: the Kissmark Trout's scar "where something let go", the pike's clean cut versus a frayed line, "Set itself", "Something ancient.", and Hob's facts and their order.

**What could be lost without anyone noticing:**
- **The dry jokes.** Many lore lines already fit. Keep them; rewrite only the fantastical ones.
- **Copies.** Lore is copied in the field guide page, and many screen strings have a hidden spare copy in the code. Change both, or the old words come back. The drowned-kingdom story lives in the intro film; keep it there.
- **Exact numbers on the Odds sheet.** Keen players like them. Keep them on a second, quieter line.
- **The Bell Pool map line,** "The moon looks in on the old bell." It is lovely, but it names her landmark, so it can only appear after she is found.

## Decided

The designer approved all four on 21 November 2026.

1. **Fish lore describes the real animal** in plain words, with at most one small touch of the drowned kingdom. The Gloom leaves the fish lore. The good dry jokes stay.
2. **"Hold" means only your fish store.** The tackle stat that keeps a fish on is called **Grip**.
3. **"Journal" everywhere,** and never "log".
4. **Old survival words leave the screens:** descent, trials, Shrine, Waystone, "survive". "New Descent" becomes "New Game".
